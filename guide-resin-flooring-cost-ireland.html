How much does resin flooring cost in Ireland?

Most commercial and industrial resin floors in Ireland cost somewhere between €20 and €130 per m² plus VAT. Where your floor lands depends on the system, the state of the concrete, the size of the area and how fast the site needs to be back in use. This guide breaks the numbers down so you can budget and compare quotes properly.

What pushes the price up or down

1. The size of the area

Big open areas are the cheapest per m². Setup, travel, mixing and curing time are much the same whether a room is 20 m² or 200 m², so small jobs and lots of small rooms (toilets, changing rooms, corridors) cost more per m². Very small jobs usually carry a minimum charge.

2. The condition of the concrete

This is the biggest unknown on most jobs. Things that add cost:

  • Old coatings, paint or tile adhesive that has to come off first
  • Cracks, spalling and broken joints that need repairing
  • Uneven floors that need levelling before the resin goes down
  • Damp slabs, which may need a moisture-tolerant primer or a damp-proof membrane
  • Oil-soaked concrete in garages and plant areas, which needs extra cleaning or grinding

Proper floor preparation (shot-blasting or diamond grinding) is normally included in the prices above. It’s the step that decides whether the floor lasts.

3. The system and thickness

A two-coat epoxy and a 9 mm PU screed are completely different products. Thicker systems use more material and take longer to lay, but they last far longer under forklifts, hot water and chemicals. Our epoxy vs PU vs MMA guide explains which one suits which site.

4. Detail work

Coving (the curved skirting where the floor meets the wall), drain and channel detailing, upstands, plinths, stairs, expansion joints and line markings are usually priced per metre or per item on top of the main floor area.

5. Access and timing

Working nights or weekends, phasing a job so part of the site keeps running, or working in a cold building that needs heating all add cost. A rapid-cure system like MMA costs more per m², but it can save money overall if every day of shutdown costs you production.

How to compare resin flooring quotes

Two quotes for “an epoxy floor” can be for very different things. Before you compare prices, check that each quote tells you:

  • The exact system and thickness (e.g. “2 mm self-levelling epoxy”, not just “epoxy floor”)
  • How the floor will be prepared: shot-blasting or grinding, not just “clean and prime”
  • Whether moisture testing is included, and what happens if the slab is damp
  • Number of coats and whether an anti-slip finish is included
  • Coving, joints, drains and line marking: included or extra?
  • Repairs: allowed for, or charged as extras once work starts?
  • When the floor can be used for foot traffic and for vehicles
  • VAT: flooring work is usually charged at 13.5% VAT. If you’re a main contractor, the reverse charge normally applies to subcontract work.

Why the cheapest quote often costs more

The quickest way to cut a resin flooring price is to skip the preparation or lay the system thinner than it should be. The floor looks fine on handover day, then starts peeling, wearing through or lifting under hot tyres within a year or two. By then you’re paying to strip it off and do the job again, plus another round of downtime.

If one quote is far cheaper than the others, ask what system and thickness it’s for and how the floor will be prepared. The answer usually explains the gap. See resin flooring vs floor paint for more on this.

Frequently asked questions

Is resin flooring cheaper than tiles?

For industrial and commercial floors, usually yes, especially over large areas. A resin coating on sound concrete costs less than quarry tiles, and it has no grout lines to crack or harbour dirt. Heavy-duty PU screeds cost about the same as good commercial tiling, but they’re seamless and quicker to lay.

Do you have a minimum charge?

Yes, for very small jobs. Setup, travel and curing time are the same whether the area is 10 m² or 60 m², so small areas are priced as a job rather than strictly per m². Call or WhatsApp us with the size and we’ll tell you straight away.

Is floor preparation included in the price?

In our quotes, yes. Shot-blasting or diamond grinding is part of every resin floor we lay, because it’s what makes the resin bond. Extra repairs, levelling or removing old coatings are listed separately so you can see exactly what you’re paying for.

How long does a resin floor last?

It depends on the system and how hard the floor is used. A thin coating in a busy warehouse may need a fresh topcoat after a few years. A properly laid PU screed or self-levelling system can last well over ten years. Good preparation matters more than anything else.

Yes. Send us the rough size, what the area is used for and a few photos of the floor, and we’ll come back with advice and a price. For larger jobs we’ll usually want to see the floor first.
